## "So... what program do you use?"

It's the classic icebreaker. Whether it's a potential client or a fresh editor looking for the "secret sauce," this question always tops the list. I get it—we love gear. But there's a subtle implication in the question that drives me a little crazy: the idea that the machine is driving the project, and I'm just along for the ride.

Let's put it this way: I'd much rather watch a masterpiece by **Alfred Hitchcock** edited on a clunky, physical **Moviola** than a three-hour explosion-fest by Michael Bay, even if he had a literal bay of top-tier AVID workstations at his disposal. 

The story happens in the brain; the software just helps it exit the building.

### The Tool is Incidental

We're living in a wild era for video. **Henry Daubrez**, an AI filmmaker who's been making waves lately, recently admitted he edited his short film *Electric Pink* using a basic screen capture app. Meanwhile, my nephews and niece are producing content on **CapCut** that has better pacing than half the commercials on TV.

The next generation of NLEs (Non-Linear Editors) is clearly going to be AI-forward. We're already seeing it. But even when the software can "auto-cut" a scene, it won't know *why* a frame-long pause on a character's face makes a viewer feel a knot in their stomach. 

**The human is what matters. The tools are just overhead.**

### My "Swiss Army Knife" Approach

Because I believe the tool should serve the project (and not the other way around), I've spent my career becoming a bit of a polyglot. I've bounced between:

* **DaVinci Resolve:** The all-around solution for collaborative cloud work and incredible color.
* **Descript:** Amazing when it comes to quick captions and reels (and the eyeliner correction is a bonus).
* **Premiere Pro & After Effects:** The reliable workhorses for heavy lifting and motion.
* **Final Cut Pro:** For when speed and fluidity are the priority.
* **And countless others...**

I don't have a "ride or die" software. I'm committed to the edit, not the brand. I like knowing the whole toolkit so that when a client brings me a vision, I can pick the exact right scalpel for the job. 

Whether it's a high-end commercial or an AI-experimental short, the goal is the same: make them feel something. Everything else is just a shortcut.
